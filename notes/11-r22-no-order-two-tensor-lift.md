# Note 11 — The `R(2,2)` swap cannot be an order-two tensor symmetry

**Status:** `CANDIDATE-NEW` theorem / proof chain internally closed / external originality and convention audit still required  
**Date:** 2026-08-17

---

## 0. Statement

Let `C` be a fusion category whose Grothendieck ring is the self-dual rank-six
fusion ring `R(2,2)` with simple objects

\[
\{1,g,h,k=gh,X,Y\}
\]

and based-ring involution

\[
\tau|_{\{1,g,h,k\}}=\mathrm{id},
\qquad
\tau(X)=Y,
\quad
\tau(Y)=X.
\]

### Candidate-new theorem

\[
\boxed{
\text{The based-ring involution }\tau
\text{ cannot lift to an order-two tensor autoequivalence of }\mathcal C.
}
\]

Equivalently, if `R(2,2)` is categorifiable, any categorification necessarily
breaks this visible order-two symmetry at the tensor level.

This is **not** yet a proof that `R(2,2)` is not categorifiable.  The next
problem is whether categorical coherence forces the based-ring symmetry to
lift.  If it does, the theorem above immediately gives non-categorifiability.

---

## 1. Why there are only two pointed restriction branches

Let

\[
\mathcal P\simeq\mathrm{Vec}_{V_4}^{\omega}
\]

be the pointed subcategory.  Since `tau` fixes every pointed simple object, the
object permutation of any tensor lift restricts to the identity automorphism of
`V4`.

A monoidal structure on the identity-on-objects functor of a pointed category
is classified, modulo monoidal natural isomorphism, by

\[
H^2(V_4,\mathbb C^\times)\cong C_2.
\]

Thus there are exactly two restriction classes:

1. the trivial class;
2. the unique nontrivial class.

`experiments/r22/pointed_action_branches.py` uses the representatives

\[
\eta_0(a,b)=1,
\qquad
\eta_1(a,b)=(-1)^{a_1b_2}
\]

and verifies that the commutator bicharacter of `eta_1` is nontrivial.

Assume from now on that an order-two categorical action generated by a tensor
lift of `tau` exists, and let

\[
\mathcal D=\mathcal C^{C_2}.
\]

The free orbit `{X,Y}` becomes a single simple object `rho` with

\[
\operatorname{FPdim}(\rho)=2(2+\sqrt6)=4+2\sqrt6.
\]

The four fixed invertibles each have two equivariant structures, so `D` has
exactly eight invertible simple objects.  Dimension then forces

\[
\boxed{
\rho^2=\sum_{u\in G(\mathcal D)}u+8\rho.
}
\]

Hence `D` is a near-group category of level `8` and group of invertibles of
order `8`.

---

## 2. The trivial `H^2` branch

If the pointed monoidal class is trivial, the eight invertibles in the
equivariantization commute.  The finite calculation in
`experiments/r22/symmetry_lift_profile.py` gives

\[
G(\mathcal D)\cong C_2^3
\quad\text{or}\quad
C_4\times C_2.
\]

### 2.1 The `C2^3+8` case is impossible

Schopieray's categorifiability theorem for elementary abelian `2`-groups
excludes a near-group categorification of type

\[
C_2^3+8.
\]

So any categorifiable target in this branch would have to be

\[
(C_4\times C_2)+8.
\]

### 2.2 Every `(C4 x C2)+8` center has one nontrivial invertible boson

Rowell--Solomon--Zhang describe all four inequivalent near-group categories of
this type.  Their center formulas show that all invertible central simples are
`a_g`, and `a_g` is a boson precisely when

\[
2g=0,
\qquad
\langle g,g\rangle=1.
\]

For `G=C2 x C4`, the four monoidal classes use the two bicharacter parameters
`m=+1,-1`.  `experiments/r22/near_group_bosons.py` computes exactly, for the
three nontrivial order-two elements,

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
g&(1,0)&(0,2)&(1,2)\\\hline
\langle g,g\rangle&-1&+1&-1.
\end{array}
\]

Therefore every one of the four centers has exactly one nontrivial invertible
boson:

\[
\boxed{a_{(0,2)}.}
\]

Hence there is a unique Tannakian `Rep(C2)` generated by an invertible simple
object.

### 2.3 Universal de-equivariantization gives `R'(2)`

Meng--Yu Corollary 5.8 has the universal quantifier:

> for **any** near-group fusion category `D` of type `C2 x C4 + 8`, there is a
> fusion category `A` with
> \[
> D\simeq A^{C_2},
> \qquad K_0(A)=R'(2).
> \]

Equivariantization and de-equivariantization are inverse once the canonical
Tannakian `Rep(C2) subset Z(D)` is specified.  Since the nontrivial invertible
boson of `Z(D)` is unique, the canonical Tannakian subcategory arising from the
hypothetical action on `C` must be the same one used by the universal
Meng--Yu de-equivariantization.

Thus de-equivariantizing `D` recovers both

\[
\mathcal C
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathcal A,
\]

so they are tensor equivalent.  But

\[
K_0(\mathcal C)=R(2,2)
\neq
R'(2)=K_0(\mathcal A),
\]

because the former is self-dual while the latter is non-self-dual.

Contradiction.

Therefore the trivial pointed `H^2` branch is impossible.

---

## 3. The nontrivial `H^2` branch

For the unique nontrivial class in

\[
H^2(V_4,\mathbb C^\times),
\]

the commutator bicharacter is nontrivial.  Consequently the eight invertibles
of `D=C^{C2}` form a **nonabelian** central extension

\[
1\longrightarrow C_2
\longrightarrow G(\mathcal D)
\longrightarrow V_4
\longrightarrow1.
\]

The only possibilities are

\[
D_8
\quad\text{or}\quad
Q_8.
\]

But the nonabelian near-group level bound cited in Schopieray's
*Categorification of integral group rings extended by one dimension* says that
if the invertible group `G` is nonabelian and a near-group ring is
categorifiable, then

\[
\ell<|G|.
\]

Here

\[
\ell=8=|G|,
\]

so neither

\[
D_8+8
\quad\text{nor}\quad
Q_8+8
\]

is categorifiable.

Therefore the nontrivial pointed `H^2` branch is impossible as well.

---

## 4. Conclusion

Both monoidal restriction classes of an identity-on-pointed-objects tensor
lift have been excluded.  Hence:

\[
\boxed{
K_0(\mathcal C)=R(2,2)
\Longrightarrow
\tau:X\leftrightarrow Y
\text{ is not the object action of an order-two tensor autoequivalence.}
}
\]

The striking consequence is that any hypothetical categorification must
**spontaneously break a manifest based-ring symmetry**.

This is a materially stronger statement than the ordinary necessary tests
recorded by Meng--Yu, but it does not yet settle categorifiability.

---

## 5. Audit boundary

Before presenting the theorem externally, the following should be checked by
an expert or independent formalization:

1. conventions identifying the nontrivial pointed monoidal class with the
   commutator extension used in the equivariantization;
2. the standard inverse equivalence between equivariantization and
   de-equivariantization with the canonical central Tannakian subcategory;
3. applicability of the quoted nonabelian near-group level bound in the
   general pseudo-unitary fusion-category setting, not only in a unitary
   subclass;
4. originality: no existing source located so far states this consequence for
   `R(2,2)`.

Until this audit is complete, label the result `CANDIDATE-NEW`, not
`CLASSICAL`.

---

## 6. Next bottleneck

To turn this into a solution of the open problem, one would like to prove

\[
\boxed{
K_0(\mathcal C)=R(2,2)
\Longrightarrow
\tau\text{ must lift.}
}
\]

Generalized near-group structure theory does **not** provide this implication
automatically.  Outer actions of fusion categories have their own obstruction
theory, so treating the lift as automatic would be circular.

Two workstreams are therefore justified:

### A. Local tensor-lift kernel

Use the pointed/module analysis to reduce the possible comparison between an
associator and its `tau`-pullback to the `M tensor M` sector.  The current raw
kernel is only 8 `tau`-orbits / 20 tensorator matrix parameters.  The goal is
not to assume the lift but to determine whether the pentagon equations force
this kernel to have a solution.

### B. Independent center-arithmetic obstruction

Specialize Meng--Yu's induction and FS-indicator equations to `m=n=2` and solve
the remaining finite integer/cyclotomic constraints more sharply than the
general rank-finiteness bounds.  A contradiction here would settle
non-categorifiability without proving a symmetry lift.

These two streams are independent and stay within the repository concurrency
cap.
