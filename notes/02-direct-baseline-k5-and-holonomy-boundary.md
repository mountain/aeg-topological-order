# Direct categorical baseline, K5 closure, and the F-only holonomy boundary

**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Status:** second blind-reconstruction round; no standard Fibonacci value table used  
**Implementations:** `experiments/direct_pentagon_baseline.py`, `experiments/associahedron_k5.py`

## 0. Executive result

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** An independent direct categorical implementation of the multiplicity-free pentagon, using the same frozen fusion grammar and strict-unit normalization but *not* importing the history-engine code, produces exactly the same polynomial system as `fibonacci_pentagon.py`.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** For the four-object Fibonacci benchmark the optimized direct baseline and the history-complex formulation both use five symbolic unknowns \((a,b,c,d,g)\), both produce 13 nonzero polynomial instances, and both reduce to the same 12 polynomial equations up to sign.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** Therefore the current four-object benchmark shows **no algebraic compression advantage** for the history representation.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** Extending the history complex to five identical \(\tau\) inputs produces the full 3-dimensional associahedron with

\[
\boxed{V=14,\qquad E=21,\qquad F=9=3\text{ squares}+6\text{ pentagons}.}
\]

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** Substituting the frozen blind \(F\)-solution gives identity holonomy around all nine 2-cell boundaries in both total-charge sectors \(1\) and \(\tau\).

**CLASSICAL.** This exposes a decisive boundary for the AEG interpretation: the F-only reassociation complex is a contractible associahedron with a coherent/flat local system. Nontrivial topological holonomy cannot come from pentagon-respecting reassociation alone; it must enter when exchange/braiding data are added.

(A(B(CD)_L)_K)_E.

**CLASSICAL.** In the convention implemented here, the two path amplitudes are

\[
F^{FCD}_{E;GL}\,F^{ABL}_{E;FK}

and

\[
\sum_H
F^{ABC}_{G;FH}
F^{AHD}_{E;GK}

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** `direct_pentagon_baseline.py` implements this formula directly and intentionally does not import `fibonacci_pentagon.py`; the only shared mathematical inputs are the frozen fusion rule and strict-unit convention.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** Among the \(2^9\) formal label nonuples, 50 satisfy both source- and target-history admissibility.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** Of those 50 admissible boundary configurations, 13 yield nonzero polynomial constraints.

\begin{aligned}
&a^2+bcg-1, && b(a+dg), && c(a+dg), && bc+d^2g-g^2,\\

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** This set agrees exactly, up to sign, with the nonzero entries generated by the two history-complex pentagon path differences in `notes/01-blind-fibonacci-pentagon.md`.

## 2. First representation-complexity verdict

**CLASSICAL.** A fair baseline must compare against an optimized categorical formulation, not against a dense tensor expression that ignores admissibility and strict-unit normalization.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** At four inputs, both formulations begin with the same five nontrivial symbolic quantities: one scalar \(g\) and one \(2\times2\) block \((a,b,c,d)\).

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** At four inputs, both formulations expose the same 13 nonzero polynomial instances and the same 12 polynomial forms.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** Hence there is no variable-count or equation-count compression at this benchmark.

**REFORMULATION.** The current advantage of the history representation is organizational rather than algebraic: it generates the equations by composing five local sparse edge maps on a typed rewrite complex, while the direct baseline generates them by indexing global pentagon boundary data.

**CONJECTURE.** Local sparse assembly may still matter for larger fusion rules or compilation/verification tasks, but this must be demonstrated by scaling experiments rather than inferred from the Fibonacci example.

**CLASSICAL.** The correct interim verdict is therefore **structural equivalence with no demonstrated complexity gain**.

## 3. Five-input history complex

**CLASSICAL.** Five ordered inputs have Catalan number \(C_4=14\) full binary parenthesizations.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** `associahedron_k5.py` generates all 14 parenthesizations and connects two vertices exactly when one local right/left reassociation relates them.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** The resulting graph has 21 undirected edges and every vertex has degree 3.

**COMPUTATIONAL-CLAIM.** Enumerating chordless cycles of lengths four and five yields exactly:

\[
3\text{ squares},\qquad 6\text{ pentagons}.
\]

**CLASSICAL.** These are the nine 2-faces of the 3-dimensional associahedron \(K_5\).

**REFORMULATION.** The square faces express independence of reassociations performed in disjoint subexpressions; the pentagonal faces express ordinary associator coherence on four adjacent factors.

## 5. The F-only holonomy boundary

**CLASSICAL.** An associahedron is a convex polytope and therefore contractible.

**CLASSICAL.** Mac Lane coherence implies that once the triangle and pentagon axioms hold, any two reassociation paths with the same endpoints induce the same canonical associator map.

**REFORMULATION.** In AEG language, the reassociation-history local system is flat: every closed path generated solely by F-moves has trivial gauge-invariant holonomy after the coherence 2-cells are imposed.

**CLASSICAL.** Consequently a nontrivial F-matrix is not itself a topological holonomy, and a satisfied pentagon cannot support a nonzero curvature invariant on the associahedron.

**REFORMULATION.** This removes one tempting but incorrect research direction: searching for topological order as nontrivial holonomy of the F-only parenthesization complex.

**CLASSICAL.** Nontrivial anyonic monodromy arises only after exchange/braiding is included. In ordinary categorical language the braid-group representation is assembled from F- and R-moves, with pentagon and hexagon guaranteeing consistency.

**REFORMULATION.** The corresponding AEG decomposition should therefore be kept sharp:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{rcl}
\text{F-moves} &\longleftrightarrow& \text{chart/reassociation transport},\\
\text{pentagon} &\longleftrightarrow& \text{flat coherence cell},\\
\text{R-moves} &\longleftrightarrow& \text{exchange primitive},\\
\text{hexagon} &\longleftrightarrow& \text{fusion--exchange compatibility},\\
\text{braid word} &\longleftrightarrow& \text{candidate nontrivial global holonomy}.
\end{array}}
\]

**REFORMULATION.** Thus the first plausible location for physically meaningful history holonomy is not the associahedron itself but the enlarged fusion--braid history groupoid after R-generators are admitted.

## 6. What explicit history may still preserve

**CLASSICAL.** Categorical coherence deliberately identifies all pure reassociation paths with the same endpoints.

**REFORMULATION.** The explicit history representation can still retain a *certificate* of how a canonical map was compiled from local moves, even though the resulting linear map is path independent.

**CONJECTURE.** This certificate-level structure may be useful for sparse verification, local recompilation, incremental solving, or complexity accounting even when it carries no new topological invariant.

**CONJECTURE.** A meaningful AEG gain may therefore be closer to a geometry of computation/proof certificates than to a new invariant of a fusion category at the F-only stage.

**CLASSICAL.** This possibility must be benchmarked; it should not be promoted to a success criterion without measured evidence.
