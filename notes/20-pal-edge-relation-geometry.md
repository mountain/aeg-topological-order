## 1. History graph and chirality field

\[

## 2. Relation-space lift

The useful representation is not the raw vertex vector.  Lift it to edge relations

\[
\eta_{uv}=c_uc_v,
\qquad (u,v)\in E(K_4).
\]

Thus one parity component produces

\[
\eta(c)\in\{\pm1\}^{6}.
\]

This is a degree-two relational lift: it forgets the simultaneous global sign flip `c -> -c` and retains only relative chirality along history edges.

That quotient is exactly appropriate for the orbit-level ambiguity: the two constant assignments `++++` and `----` have the same edge relation state.

## 3. Projective coherent predicate

Let

\[
\mathbf 1_E=(1,1,1,1,1,1)
\]

be the fully coherent edge predicate.  Define the normalized Hermitian/projective overlap

\[
q(c)
=
\frac{|\langle\mathbf1_E,\eta(c)\rangle|^2}
{\|\mathbf1_E\|^2\,\|\eta(c)\|^2}.
\]

Since every entry is `+/-1`, this is simply

\[
q(c)=\frac{(\sum_{e\in E}\eta_e)^2}{36}.
\]

For the sixteen chirality fields on one `K_4`, exact enumeration gives only three values:

\[
\boxed{
q=
\begin{cases}
1,& c\text{ is constant};\\
1/9,& c\text{ has two plus and two minus vertices};\\
0,& c\text{ has one versus three signs}.
\end{cases}}
\]

Equivalently, if `cut(c)` is the number of edges joining opposite signs, then

\[
\sum_e\eta_e=6-2\,\mathrm{cut}(c),
\]

so the projective observable is a function of the history cut geometry.

## 4. Two parity components

For the complete eight-bit field define

\[
Q(c)=\min(q_{\rm even}(c),q_{\rm odd}(c)).
\]

Exact enumeration of all `256` assignments gives

\[
\boxed{
Q=0\text{ on }192\text{ assignments},
\qquad
Q=1/9\text{ on }60,
\qquad
Q=1\text{ on }4.}
\]

The four `Q=1` masks, in the repository's lexicographic bit convention, are

\[
\boxed{0,105,150,255}.
\]

They are precisely the assignments constant on each of the two parity transport orbits.
